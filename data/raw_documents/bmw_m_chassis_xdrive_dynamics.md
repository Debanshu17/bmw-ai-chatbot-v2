# BMW M Chassis and M xDrive Vehicle Dynamics Manual

## Chapter 1: M xDrive All-Wheel Drive and Active M Differential
The M xDrive all-wheel drive system is engineered specifically for high-performance track driving. It employs an electronically controlled multi-plate clutch inside a central transfer case that distributes drive torque continuously and variably between the front and rear axles. Under normal cruising conditions, 100% of the drive torque is directed to the rear wheels to maximize fuel efficiency and preserve traditional rear-wheel drive steering feel. Front-wheel drive torque is engaged within milliseconds only when rear slip is detected. The system offers three selectable driving modes: 4WD (default stability biased), 4WD Sport (increased torque distribution to rear axle for controlled oversteer), and 2WD (complete front-axle decoupling with DSC fully disengaged). The rear axle integrates the Active M Differential with an electro-mechanical multi-plate locking clutch capable of generating a 0 to 100 percent locking rate within 150 milliseconds.

## Chapter 2: Adaptive M Suspension and Kinematic Geometry
The front suspension features an M-specific double-joint spring strut axle with aluminum wishbones and forged control arms to minimize unsprung mass. The front axle incorporates an increased caster angle of 8.5 degrees and a negative camber of -1.3 degrees to deliver sharp turn-in response and linear steering feedback. The rear axle utilizes a lightweight five-link suspension featuring rigid rubber-metal bushings and ball joints on all control arms for deflection-free toe and camber stability under maximum lateral load. Adaptive M Damping is standard, employing electronically controlled shock absorbers with internal electromagnetic valves. Damping forces are calculated and adjusted continuously at each individual wheel in intervals of 2.5 milliseconds using sensor data measuring wheel travel, steering angle, vehicle speed, and yaw rate.

## Chapter 3: Braking Systems and Carbon-Ceramic Specifications
BMW M vehicles are equipped with two distinct high-performance braking options. The standard M Compound brakes feature ventilated and perforated cast iron discs connected to aluminum hats by cast-in stainless steel friction pins, reducing thermal expansion distortion. The optional M Carbon Ceramic braking system (identifiable by gold-painted calipers) provides significant thermal resilience and weight savings. The front carbon-ceramic discs measure 400 mm in diameter and 38 mm in thickness, clamped by six-piston fixed calipers. The rear carbon-ceramic discs measure 380 mm in diameter and 28 mm in thickness, clamped by single-piston floating calipers with integrated electronic parking brake actuators. Compared to standard steel brakes, the carbon ceramic package saves 14.3 kg (31.5 lbs) of rotating unsprung mass and withstands continuous operating temperatures up to 1,000 degrees Celsius without thermal fading.

## Chapter 4: Integrated Brake-by-Wire Control
The braking system utilizes an integrated electro-mechanical brake actuation system (Brake-by-Wire) that combines brake actuation, booster, and dynamic stability control functions into a single compact hydraulic unit. A precision force sensor detects the driver pedal input, and a high-speed electric motor builds hydraulic pressure in under 100 milliseconds. The system provides two driver-selectable pedal feel settings via the M Setup menu: Comfort mode provides a progressive, compliant pedal travel for everyday driving, while Sport mode provides immediate pedal bite, firm resistance, and ultra-short pedal travel tailored for threshold braking on track circuits.
