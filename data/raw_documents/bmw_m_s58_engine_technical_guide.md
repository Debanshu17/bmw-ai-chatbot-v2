# BMW M S58 Twin-Turbocharged Engine Technical Guide

## Chapter 1: Engine Architecture and Core Specifications
The BMW S58 engine is a high-performance 3.0-liter (2,993 cc) inline-six cylinder engine developed by BMW M GmbH, succeeding the S55 powerplant. The engine has a cylinder bore of 84.0 mm and a piston stroke of 90.0 mm. It utilizes a compression ratio of 9.3:1 to optimize thermal efficiency under forced induction. The cylinder block features a closed-deck design that provides maximum rigidity, allowing it to withstand extreme combustion pressures up to 140 bar. The cylinder walls are coated via twin-wire arc spraying (LDS) with an ultra-thin iron coating to reduce friction and eliminate traditional cylinder liners. A forged, lightweight chrome-molybdenum steel crankshaft (42CrMo4) reduces rotational inertia and allows the engine to rev smoothly up to an electronically limited redline of 7,200 RPM. The 3D-printed cylinder head core allows coolant ducts to be routed with geometric freedom that traditional casting cannot achieve.

## Chapter 2: Twin-Turbocharging and Air Induction System
Forced induction on the S58 engine is delivered by two mono-scroll turbochargers with integrated wastegates. Turbocharger 1 supplies compressed air to cylinders 1, 2, and 3, while Turbocharger 2 supplies cylinders 4, 5, and 6. In the standard specification, the system delivers a peak boost pressure of 1.7 bar (24.7 psi). In Competition and CS models, the electronic wastegate controls boost pressure up to a maximum rating of 2.1 bar (30.5 psi). The intake air is cooled via an indirect charge air cooling system utilizing a dedicated low-temperature coolant circuit and a liquid-to-air intercooler positioned directly atop the engine intake manifold. This short intake runner design reduces turbocharger lag to less than 0.3 seconds during tip-in throttle transients.

## Chapter 3: Fuel Injection and Valvetrain Technology
The S58 utilizes BMW High Precision Injection (HPI) direct fuel injection operating at a maximum fuel rail pressure of 350 bar (5,076 psi). Multi-hole solenoid injectors deliver precise spray patterns directly over the recessed piston bowls to suppress pre-ignition (knock). Variable gas exchange is governed by VALVETRONIC fully variable valve timing and Double-VANOS variable camshaft timing. The VALVETRONIC system alters intake valve lift continuously between 0.2 mm and 9.9 mm, replacing conventional throttle body restriction during part-load operation. The exhaust camshaft provides up to 55 degrees of crank angle adjustment, while the intake camshaft allows 70 degrees of adjustment.

## Chapter 4: Track-Ready Lubrication and Cooling Systems
The lubrication system of the S58 is engineered to withstand track driving with sustained lateral and longitudinal accelerations exceeding 1.2g. It utilizes an aluminum oil pan featuring two distinct chambers: a smaller front sump and a larger rear sump. A map-controlled variable displacement oil pump is augmented by an auxiliary suction stage that scavenges oil from the smaller chamber back into the main sump under hard braking and cornering. The cooling architecture comprises an ultra-efficient central high-temperature radiator, two auxiliary side-mounted radiators in the wheel arches, a dedicated engine oil cooler mounted horizontally at the bottom of the front bumper, and a separate transmission oil cooler for the 8-speed M Steptronic gearbox. Total engine oil capacity is 7.0 liters of BMW Longlife-12 FE 0W-30 synthetic lubricant.
